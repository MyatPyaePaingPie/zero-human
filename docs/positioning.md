# Positioning: hackathon -> business

The claim: **we turn hackathon projects into businesses.** Not "we review your pitch". Every hackathon
project dies on Monday because nobody tells the team the four things it is missing. We tell them,
exactly, and hand their coding agent the fixes.

## The four gaps between a hackathon project and a business
1. **Someone who pays.** Named payer, painful job, current spend, price. (clarity + demand + viability
   lenses, humans on top)
2. **A way to take money.** A live page a stranger can find, trust, and buy from. (objective probes:
   found, trusted, buyable; agent-fixable)
3. **Proof the claim survives strangers.** Real people from outside the team say what it is and whether
   they would pay. (human layer, blind then reveal)
4. **A loop.** Fix, re-run, see the delta; every run sharper than the last. (agent.md + compounding)

The report is organized as those four gaps, each with a stamp. The overall stamp reads
"ready to charge" / "one gap away" / "not a business yet, here is why".

## Why the claim is big and defensible
- Hackathons are a repeatable, weekly, worldwide channel with thousands of projects each and no
  post-event product. Organizers want a "what happened to the projects" story. Sponsors want leads.
- Every project reviewed is a labeled data point on what separates projects that make money from
  those that do not. Re-run at 30 days and ask "did you charge anyone?" and the corpus becomes the
  only dataset of hackathon-to-business outcomes. That is the moat, not the model.
- The output is executable by the team's own agent, so the gap between "told what to do" and "did it"
  is one paste.

## Today's moves (room-scale)
- **Sweep this hackathon.** Reality-check every submission we can find (the sweep already does this
  for PH launches). Every team gets a link to its own agent.md; the room gets a leaderboard by stamp.
  Demo, data, and leads in one run.
- **Landing line (Myat, #6):** "Have a hackathon project? Want it to be a real business? Paste the repo,
  the page, the deck. We tell you exactly what to do next." Keep "stop bullshitting yourself".
- **QR in the room** to the paste box. Ask one question at intake: "will you re-run in 30 days?" and
  take an email/phone only for that (no accounts).

## Later (bigger businesses / ongoing support)
- Weekly re-run subscription: the Monday-after product. Agent fixes, humans re-judge, delta chart.
- Organizer package: reality-check every submission at the event; sponsors pay.
- Outcome tracking: 30/90-day "did you charge anyone" follow-up feeds the priors.
